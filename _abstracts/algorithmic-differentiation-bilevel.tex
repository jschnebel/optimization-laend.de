\begin{center}
  {\Large\bfseries Deep Equilibrium Models via Mirror Descent: on the choice of the Bregman geometry}
\end{center}

\bigskip

\noindent
Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) are popular techniques often used in the context of image reconstruction to learn image regularizers as fixed points of an optimization algorithm. Their convergence usually relies on the contractivity of Euclidean (proximal) gradient steps, but this breaks down when the data term is not Euclidean-smooth, for instance in the case of image reconstruction under Poisson noise, where the fidelity is a Kullback--Leibler divergence. In this case we turn to Mirror Descent whose iteration becomes the building block of a DEQ with a learned, nonconvex regularizer. Its convergence is studied through relative smoothness and a Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz analysis adapted to non-closed domains. A central question then arises: which mirror map should one use? We discuss several common choices such as Burg's/shifted Burg's and Boltzmann--Shannon entropies, discussing for each gains and costs, both in theory and in practice.
